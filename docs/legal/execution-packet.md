# Privacy and incident operating procedure

Draft runbook for the accountable operator. It must be exercised against actual storage and service controls; prose is not evidence of implementation.

For a privacy request, use a private channel. Record a minimal request identifier and scope, not the private content in a public issue. Ask only for information proportionate to locating the record or establishing authority. Do not demand identity documents as a default. Identify active storage, exported copies and backup constraints. Complete the applicable response and record the actual result; determine legal deadlines from the requester's circumstances and applicable law.

For an incident, contain the affected path without unnecessarily taking down independent tag checks. Revoke affected access, preserve necessary evidence privately and stop collection if safe retention or withdrawal cannot be maintained. Record discovered facts, uncertain facts, affected data categories and systems, containment and responsible owner. Avoid logging secret values or unredacted submissions.

Assess notification obligations with qualified advice. Do not promise a universal 72-hour response or breach notification period. Inform affected people accurately when required, and communicate practical steps. A public incident summary should protect people and remediation details while stating scope, impact, correction and remaining uncertainty.

Restore to an isolated environment. Apply expiry and deletion instructions before restoring application access or analysis. Verify access, retention, backup age and deletion behavior; a successful database restore alone is insufficient. Close the incident only with evidence and an owner review. Preserve required legal holds with restricted access and explicit reasons, rather than silently extending research retention.
